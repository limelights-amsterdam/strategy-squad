# Provocative questions — the facilitator's method

The squad's analysis is *input*, not the deliverable. The deliverable for a strategist-facilitator is
the **set of questions they walk into the room with** — the questions nobody else dares to ask, that
make a leadership team confront what it's avoiding. This turns "too brutal to show the client" raw
critique into a usable instrument.

## What makes a question land in a leadership off-site
- **It targets an unspoken assumption**, not a fact. ("What would have to be true about [X] for this
  plan to work — and do we actually believe that?")
- **It's neutral on the surface, lethal underneath.** It doesn't accuse; it invites the room to say the
  uncomfortable thing themselves.
- **It's specific to this case.** Generic ("are we being ambitious enough?") dies. Named ("we're
  assuming Channel B converts at 2× Channel A — who here has seen that hold?") bites.
- **It's owned-answerable.** Someone in the room can actually answer it; it isn't rhetorical fog.
- **It opens a conversation**, not a yes/no. Calibrated "what / how" beats "do you / will you".

## Transform: critique → question
For each brutal challenge from the roast:
1. Find the **buried assumption** underneath the critique.
2. Phrase it as a **calibrated question** the room can't dodge ("How would we know if…", "What are we
   assuming about…", "What happens to this plan if…").
3. Note **why it stings** (what it forces into the open).
4. Add a **follow-up** for when the room deflects (the second question that closes the escape route).

## Grouping
Cluster the questions into 3-5 themes (e.g. *the assumption we're not testing*, *the choice we're
avoiding*, *the number nobody owns*, *the bet we're too scared to name*). Order them for the room:
open with one that builds safety, save the sharpest for when the group is warm.

## Tone guardrails
The facilitator asks to *help the room think*, not to win. Every question should be askable out loud
without putting a specific person on trial. Hard on the strategy, soft on the people.
